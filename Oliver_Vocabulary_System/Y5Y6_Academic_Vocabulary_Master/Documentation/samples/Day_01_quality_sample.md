## Section 1: New Vocabulary
### 1. analyse (verb)
- Definition: To look closely at something so you can understand how it works or what it means.
- Synonyms: examine, study, investigate
- Antonyms: ignore, overlook
- Word Family: analyse, analysis, analytical, analytically, analyst
- Common Collocations: analyse data; analyse a text; analyse the results
- Example Sentence: In science, we analyse the data from our plant experiment before writing a conclusion.
- Creative writing: Basic: We looked at the results. / Improved: We analysed the results to explain why some seeds grew faster.

### 2. significant (adjective)
- Definition: Important enough to notice or to make a real difference.
- Synonyms: important, notable, considerable
- Antonyms: minor, insignificant
- Word Family: significant, significance, significantly, insignificant
- Common Collocations: significant difference; significant impact; statistically significant
- Example Sentence: There was a significant rise in recycling after the school launched its new bins.
- Creative writing: Basic: It was a big change. / Improved: It was a significant change that improved the whole programme.

### 3. environment (noun)
- Definition: The natural world around us, or the conditions in which people or animals live.
- Synonyms: surroundings, habitat, setting
- Antonyms: —
- Word Family: environment, environmental, environmentally, environmentalist
- Common Collocations: natural environment; learning environment; protect the environment
- Example Sentence: Protecting the local environment means keeping creeks clean and habitats safe for wildlife.
- Creative writing: Basic: We should look after nature. / Improved: We should protect the environment by reducing plastic waste.

### 4. determine (verb)
- Definition: To work out or decide something after thinking or checking carefully.
- Synonyms: decide, establish, ascertain
- Antonyms: —
- Word Family: determine, determination, determined, determinant
- Common Collocations: determine the cause; determine whether; determine the outcome
- Example Sentence: The class used a thermometer to determine the temperature of the water.
- Creative writing: Basic: We found out the answer. / Improved: We determined the answer by testing each method.

### 5. evaluate (verb)
- Definition: To judge how good, useful, or successful something is.
- Synonyms: assess, judge, appraise
- Antonyms: —
- Word Family: evaluate, evaluation, evaluative, evaluator
- Common Collocations: evaluate evidence; evaluate a method; evaluate the effectiveness
- Example Sentence: After the debate, we evaluate which arguments were supported by strong evidence.
- Creative writing: Basic: We said if it was good. / Improved: We evaluated the project using clear criteria.

### 6. demonstrate (verb)
- Definition: To show clearly that something is true or how something works.
- Synonyms: show, prove, illustrate
- Antonyms: —
- Word Family: demonstrate, demonstration, demonstrative, demonstrator
- Common Collocations: demonstrate understanding; demonstrate a skill; clearly demonstrate
- Example Sentence: The diagram demonstrates how water moves through the water cycle.
- Creative writing: Basic: The picture shows the idea. / Improved: The diagram demonstrates the process step by step.

### 7. justify (verb)
- Definition: To give good reasons that explain why a choice or opinion is fair or correct.
- Synonyms: defend, support, explain
- Antonyms: —
- Word Family: justify, justification, justifiable, justifiably
- Common Collocations: justify a decision; justify an opinion; justify your answer
- Example Sentence: In your persuasive text, justify your opinion with facts, not only feelings.
- Creative writing: Basic: Say why you think that. / Improved: Justify your opinion with two pieces of evidence.

### 8. perspective (noun)
- Definition: A particular way of looking at or thinking about something.
- Synonyms: viewpoint, standpoint, angle
- Antonyms: —
- Word Family: perspective, perspectives
- Common Collocations: from a … perspective; historical perspective; different perspectives
- Example Sentence: From a farmer’s perspective, drought is more than a weather story—it affects livelihoods.
- Creative writing: Basic: Different people think differently. / Improved: Each character offers a different perspective on the conflict.

### 9. consequence (noun)
- Definition: Something that happens as a result of an action or decision.
- Synonyms: result, outcome, effect
- Antonyms: cause
- Word Family: consequence, consequences, consequent, consequently
- Common Collocations: serious consequence; unintended consequence; as a consequence
- Example Sentence: One consequence of cutting down trees is the loss of animal habitats.
- Creative writing: Basic: Bad things happened because of it. / Improved: A serious consequence of pollution is damage to marine life.

### 10. impact (noun)
- Definition: A strong effect that something has on a person, place, or situation.
- Synonyms: effect, influence, impression
- Antonyms: —
- Word Family: impact, impacts, impactful
- Common Collocations: positive impact; negative impact; have an impact on
- Example Sentence: Tourism can have a positive impact on local jobs, but also a negative impact on fragile reefs.
- Creative writing: Basic: It changed things a lot. / Improved: The new law had a lasting impact on water safety.

## Section 3: Mini Reading — The Ridge Beyond the Marked Trail
Theme: adventure · 183 words

On the third morning of school camp, the Year 6 group left the painted markers and climbed toward a ridge the map showed only as a pale smudge. They had been told to notice the weather, the rock, and the way a person speaks when the path grows steep. A thin wind moved through the gums. Nobody treated the walk as a race. Ms Hart asked them to investigate the slope before anyone crossed the loose stone. A patient method mattered more than speed. Oliver paused and tried to determine which trees still held last night's rain, then compare the wet bark with the dry dust. The group had to evaluate whether the safer line was longer but sound, or shorter and likely to crumble. The crumbling edge was one consequence they could not ignore. They did not need a heroic tale. They needed to analyse what they had seen so the next pair would not slip. The change underfoot was significant enough to slow the line. By the fire trail, the better prize was this: they could describe the ridge without exaggeration.
